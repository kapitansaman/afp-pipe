Use the supplied approved source findings alongside well-established qualitative model knowledge; preserve numerical values, units, source limitations and distinctions between germination, emergence and crop quality. Translate technical terminology into natural Persian, not literal invented words.

Where the approved commercial matrix supports it, explain the practical irrigation connection and add a restrained invitation using only the supplied authorized product links. Unit-display policy: keep original factual quantities/units in the evidence and raw model response; do not guess or perform conversions in the LLM. A deterministic publication-rendering layer converts Fahrenheit to Celsius and US measures to metric for fa-IR, tg-TJ, ar-IQ and ru-RU; en-US displays Fahrenheit only for temperature and US customary units with metric equivalents in parentheses for other supported quantities. No Celsius parenthesis in English. Preserve absolute-temperature versus temperature-difference meaning; ambiguous gallons, thermal accumulation or unsupported compound units require review. Publication display conversions are separate from evidence fidelity.

Mixed knowledge: model knowledge and reasoning are useful, not ignored. Use established qualitative definitions, explanations, non-prescriptive practical guidance and limited synthesis without inventing source attribution. Not every explanation needs field research or a citation. Loaded source claims and quantities retain their original evidence and limits; uncertain, current, local, numeric or safety-critical prescriptions require suitable verification. In source extraction never place model memory in a literal evidence/quote field. In review do not reject permitted general background solely because the retrieved excerpts omit it; flag actual contradiction, unsupported sensitive advice or unresolved uncertainty.

Organization: lead with the answer, then use coherent main/subheadings, relevant factors and practical guidance, a useful table, a proportionate product connection and a concise conclusion. Sources and FAQ belong at the end through the renderer. Do not duplicate headings or the title, repeat keywords/caveats, pad paragraphs or force a comprehensive template onto a narrow question. Semantic headings support the theme’s table of contents; no generated navigation scripts.
